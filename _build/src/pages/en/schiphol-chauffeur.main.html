Commission No. I · Schiphol

Chauffeur at Schiphol

A chauffeur service for arrivals and departures at Amsterdam Airport Schiphol, kept by a house that sits minutes from the runway.

You are met inside the terminal by a chauffeur who already knows your name, your flight and where the day goes next.

The flight is watched from Aalsmeer. The car is there before you are.

An airport transfer is the shortest commission we accept, and it is prepared like the longest. The chauffeur is briefed by name, the car is presented the night before, and the timings are written against your flight rather than the timetable.

The arrival

Flights are tracked, so an early landing or a long delay changes nothing at your end — a back-up chauffeur is held ready either way. You are met inside the terminal, not at a kerb. Luggage is taken from you and the car is a short walk away.

Waiting time of sixty minutes runs from touchdown, which leaves room for passport control and the baggage hall without anyone watching a clock.

The departure

Collection is from the residence, the office or the hotel, at a time worked back from the flight and the road that morning. Doors are opened, cases are carried, and you are set down at the departures door rather than somewhere near it.

Notice

A day is usually enough for a Schiphol transfer. A particular car, a larger party or a delegation is better placed further ahead.

Scheduled arrivals

Met inside the terminal

One chauffeur, briefed on the party and the flight, waiting inside before the doors open. Dutch and English as standard; other languages by arrangement, given notice.

Private aviation

General Aviation Terminal

Arrivals at the General Aviation Terminal at Schiphol-Oost are met in the same manner, timed against the aircraft rather than a schedule. Flight departments and handlers reach one number.

Onward by road

Amsterdam · The Hague

Most arrivals go on to Amsterdam, the Zuidas or The Hague. Others continue by road into Belgium or Germany without changing car or chauffeur.

A transfer is one part of The Chauffeur, the commission that also covers roadshows, events and multi-day work.

01

What happens if the flight is delayed?

Nothing changes at your end. Flights are tracked from the house, the chauffeur's timing moves with the aircraft, and a back-up chauffeur is held ready in case a long delay runs into the next commitment.

02

Where does the chauffeur meet us at Schiphol?

Inside the terminal, not at a kerb. The exact meeting point is written into the proposal, and the chauffeur's name and number reach you before you fly.

03

How long will the chauffeur wait?

Sixty minutes of waiting time run from touchdown. If passport control or baggage takes longer, tell us on the one number and the car is held.

04

How far ahead should a Schiphol transfer be arranged?

A day is usually enough. A particular car, a larger party or a delegation is better placed further ahead, because we hold a limited number of commissions at a time.

05

How is a Schiphol transfer priced?

Against the day rather than the distance. Vehicle, chauffeur, timings and cost arrive in one written proposal, and nothing is added afterwards without agreement.
